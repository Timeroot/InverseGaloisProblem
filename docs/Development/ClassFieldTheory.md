# The class field theory layer

**Status: 2026-08-22.** Everything described here is sorry-free and axiom-free
(`#print axioms` gives `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]` on every capstone).
The layer lives in `InverseGalois/CFT/` and is indexed, declaration by declaration, in the module
docstring of `InverseGalois/CFT.lean`. **This document is the strategic companion to that index**:
it says what is *mathematically* available, what is deliberately absent, and what the next steps
cost. Read `InverseGalois/CFT.lean` for the inventory, read this for the map.

The layer exists to feed the Shafarevich programme (`docs/Development/Shafarevich.md`), but the
degree-two part of it now stands on its own: **Hilbert reciprocity, the Hasse–Minkowski theorem for
ternary forms, and the Hasse norm theorem for quadratic extensions of `ℚ` are all proved here.**

---

## 0. Why any of this had to be built

Mathlib contains **no class field theory**. At the pin used by this repository there is no
Chebotarev in general, no Grunwald–Wang, no Kronecker–Weber, no Hasse norm theorem, no Artin map,
no ray class groups, no ideles, no local class field theory, no Tate cohomology, no cup products,
no Herbrand quotients, no Hochschild–Serre, no maximal unramified extension, no Krasner's lemma,
no crossed products, no relative Brauer group, and no local invariant map
`inv_v : Br(K_v) → ℚ/ℤ`. `Mathlib/Algebra/BrauerGroup/Defs.lean` carries the Brauer *monoid* of
central simple algebras and nothing more: the group law, Galois descent and the uniqueness half of
Wedderburn's theorem are all absent, and `L ⊗[K] L ≃ (Gal(L/K) → L)` does not exist either. The
class `IsNonarchimedeanLocalField` (`Mathlib/NumberTheory/LocalField/Basic.lean`) exists with
**zero instances** — not even `ℚ_[p]` is one.

What Mathlib *does* supply, and what this layer leans on heavily:

* `ℚ_[p]` and `ℤ_[p]` with their norms, `PadicInt.toZModPow`, Hensel's lemma;
* the Legendre and Jacobi symbols with **quadratic reciprocity** and both supplementary laws
  (`Mathlib/NumberTheory/LegendreSymbol/`);
* **Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions**
  (`Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/PrimesInAP.lean`);
* group cohomology as a derived functor, with `H¹` and `H²` and explicit cocycle models;
* the general theory of central simple algebras, Wedderburn's *existence* theorem, and tensor
  products of algebras.

## 1. What is proved

### 1.1 Group cohomology and the Brauer group, over an arbitrary field

This is the classical crossed-product half of class field theory, and it is complete.

| statement | location |
|---|---|
| `H²(G, M)` is in bijection with extensions of `G` by `M`, and vanishes exactly when they all split | `CFT/GroupCohomology/Classification.lean` |
| corestriction in every degree, `res ≫ cor = [G : S] • id`, hence `H^n(G, –)` is killed by `|G|` for `n > 0` | `CFT/GroupCohomology/Corestriction.lean` |
| every `2`-cocycle of a finite cyclic group is cohomologous to an explicit one; `H²(cyclic) = invariants / norms` | `CFT/GroupCohomology/CyclicSurjective.lean`, `CyclicH2.lean` |
| Galois descent for modules (Speiser) | `CFT/GaloisDescent.lean` |
| the Brauer group: `A ⊗ B` central simple, `A ⊗ Aᵒᵖ ≅ Mₙ`, the group law, base change, the relative group `Br(L/K)` | `CFT/Brauer/{TensorSimple,Opposite,Group,BaseChange,Tower}.lean` |
| Skolem–Noether, and its semilinear refinement | `CFT/Brauer/{SkolemNoether,Semilinear}.lean` |
| the double centralizer theorem, `B ⊗ C_E(B) ≅ E`, a self-centralizing subfield splits | `CFT/Brauer/{Centralizer,CentralizerProduct}.lean` |
| Wedderburn uniqueness in the split case; trivial Brauer class ⇔ matrix algebra | `CFT/Brauer/Split.lean` |
| the crossed product: central simple, split by `L`, multiplicative in the cocycle, trivial exactly on coboundaries | `CFT/Brauer/CrossedProduct*.lean` |
| **`Br(L/K) ≅ H²(Gal(L/K), Lˣ)`** for every finite Galois `L/K`, hence killed by `[L : K]` | `CFT/Brauer/H2Surjective.lean` |
| **`Br(L/K) ≅ Kˣ / N_{L/K}(Lˣ)`** for `L/K` cyclic | `CFT/Brauer/CyclicNorm.lean` |

### 1.2 The local theory in degree two

The Hilbert symbol `(a, b)_K` is *defined* as the isotropy of `z² − a x² − b y²`
(`CFT/Local/HilbertSymbol.lean`), so its symmetry, its invariance under squares and its
identification with "`a` is a norm from `K(√b)`" are formal. Everything arithmetic is then a
computation of norm forms:

* **odd `p`.** A unit of `ℤ_[p]` is a square iff its residue is (`CFT/Local/PadicSquares.lean`).
  The unramified quadratic extension has norms exactly the elements of even valuation
  (`UnramifiedNormForm.lean`); the ramified ones are computed in `RamifiedNormForm.lean`. Together
  these give bimultiplicativity of `( , )_{ℚ_p}`, the explicit expression as a product of quadratic
  characters, and `[ℚ_p^× : N(L^×)] = 2` (`PadicHilbertMul.lean`), hence `Br(L/ℚ_p) ≅ ℤ/2` for
  quadratic `L`.
* **`p = 2`.** A dyadic unit is a square iff it is `≡ 1 mod 8`, and there are exactly eight square
  classes (`PadicSquaresTwo.lean`). Isotropy over `ℚ_2` is decided modulo eight, and the symbol of
  two dyadic units is `1` iff one of them is `≡ 1 mod 4` (`DyadicHilbert.lean`); the general dyadic
  symbol and its bimultiplicativity follow (`DyadicHilbertMul.lean`).
* **the real place.** `(a, b)_ℝ = −1` iff `a < 0` and `b < 0` (`HilbertSymbol.lean`).
* the resulting integer dictionary, symbols as Legendre symbols, is `Local/LegendreHilbert.lean`.

### 1.3 The global theory in degree two

| statement | location |
|---|---|
| the family `p ↦ (a, b)_{ℚ_p}` is trivial at all but finitely many `p`, so `∏_v (a,b)_v` makes sense | `Global/HilbertPlaces.lean`, `HilbertProduct.lean` |
| the product over all places is bimultiplicative | `Global/HilbertBimul.lean` |
| the product on the generating pairs `{−1} ∪ primes`: the two supplementary laws and quadratic reciprocity | `Global/OddGenerators.lean`, `TwoGenerators.lean` |
| **Hilbert reciprocity**: `∏_v (a, b)_v = 1` for all nonzero rationals `a, b` | `Global/Reciprocity.lean` |
| **Hasse–Minkowski for ternary forms over `ℚ`**: a conic with a point at every place has a rational point | `Global/HasseMinkowski.lean` |
| the real place is redundant: the finite places already force `(a,b)_ℚ = 1` | `Global/HasseMinkowski.lean` |
| **the Hasse norm theorem for `ℚ(√b)/ℚ`** = Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether for quaternion algebras over `ℚ` | `Global/HasseNorm.lean` |

The headline signatures are

```lean
theorem hilbertProduct_eq_one {a b : ℚ} (ha : a ≠ 0) (hb : b ≠ 0) :
    hilbertProduct a b = 1

theorem isHilbertIsotropic_rat_of_forall_local {a b : ℚ} (ha : a ≠ 0) (hb : b ≠ 0)
    (hreal : IsHilbertIsotropic ((a : ℝ)) ((b : ℝ)))
    (hloc : ∀ p : Nat.Primes, IsHilbertIsotropic ((a : ℚ_[(p : ℕ)])) ((b : ℚ_[(p : ℕ)]))) :
    IsHilbertIsotropic a b

theorem exists_sub_sq_iff_forall_local {a b : ℚ} (ha : a ≠ 0) (hb : b ≠ 0) :
    (∃ u v : ℚ, a = u ^ 2 - b * v ^ 2) ↔
      ∀ p : Nat.Primes, ∃ u v : ℚ_[(p : ℕ)],
        ((a : ℚ_[(p : ℕ)])) = u ^ 2 - ((b : ℚ_[(p : ℕ)])) * v ^ 2
```

#### How the descent works, and why it is not circular

`Global/HasseMinkowski.lean` is Legendre's classical descent, done by strong induction on
`|a| + |b|` for squarefree integers `a, b`.

1. Reduce `ℚ` to squarefree integers by `q = m c²` (`Global/SquarefreeCRT.lean`) and invariance of
   the symbol under squares.
2. Base case `|b| ≤ 1`: then `a, b ∈ {±1}`. If either is a square the symbol is `1`; the remaining
   case `a = b = −1` contradicts the hypothesis at the *real* place.
3. Step. Every prime `q ∣ b` has `a` a square mod `q` — automatic if `q ∣ a`, automatic for `q = 2`
   (by `decide`), and otherwise this is exactly the local hypothesis at `q` read through
   `Global/LocalSquare.lean`, which identifies the symbol at a ramified odd place with a Legendre
   symbol. Squarefreeness of `b` is what makes `q` *exactly* divide `b`.
4. Chinese remainder (`Global/SquarefreeCRT.lean`) then gives `t` with `b ∣ t² − a` and
   `2|t| ≤ |b|`; write `t² − a = b b'`.
5. The point `(1, 1, t)` shows `(a, t² − a) = 1` **in every field**, so `(a, b b') = 1` over `ℚ`
   and over every completion.
6. Cancel: the elements of symbol one against `a` are the nonzero values of the norm form of
   `ℚ(√a)`, hence a *group*. This one-sided cancellation (`Global/DescentTools.lean`) is all that
   is used — **bimultiplicativity over `ℚ` is never invoked**, which is what keeps the argument
   independent of reciprocity rather than circular with it.
7. `4|b||b'| = 4|t² − a| ≤ 4t² + 4|a| ≤ b² + 4|b| < 4b²` when `|b| ≥ 2`, so the squarefree part of
   `b'` is strictly smaller than `b` and the induction closes.

Reciprocity enters only at the very end, to *delete* the real hypothesis
(`hilbertSymbol_rat_of_forall_finite`): the product of the finite symbols is the real symbol, so if
every finite symbol is `1` then so is the real one.

## 2. What is deliberately not here

* **The local invariant map `inv_v : Br(K_v) → ℚ/ℤ`,** and with it
  `Br(K^ur/K) ≅ ℚ/ℤ`. This needs unramified extensions of a local field and the valuation-theoretic
  computation of their Brauer group. It cannot even be *stated* comfortably until `ℚ_[p]` is made
  an instance of `IsNonarchimedeanLocalField`.
* **Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether in general**, i.e. exactness of
  `0 → Br(K) → ⊕_v Br(K_v) → ℚ/ℤ → 0`. The degree-two slice of it over `ℚ` *is* proved
  (§1.3), because in degree two the whole thing degenerates to a statement about ternary quadratic
  forms. Nothing about that argument generalises to exponent `ℓ > 2`, which is the case
  Scholz–Reichardt needs.
* **Kronecker–Weber**, and more generally the gluing of prescribed local abelian characters into a
  global one (Grunwald–Wang). This is the second of the two blockers in
  `docs/Development/Shafarevich.md` §0.2.
* Ideles, ray class groups, the Artin map, Tate cohomology, the two inequalities. None of these is
  needed for anything currently in the tree.

## 3. The degree-two frontier

The natural completion of the degree-two theory is **Serre's existence theorem** (*A Course in
Arithmetic*, Ch. III, Thm 4): given finitely many `aᵢ ∈ ℚ^×` and signs `ε_{i,v} = ±1` which are
almost all `1`, satisfy `∏_v ε_{i,v} = 1`, and are locally realizable, there is `x ∈ ℚ^×` with
`(aᵢ, x)_v = ε_{i,v}` for all `i` and `v`. This is exponent-two Grunwald–Wang, and it is the
surjectivity half of the exact sequence above in degree two.

Its two ingredients are

1. **injectivity of `ℚ^×/(ℚ^×)² → ∏_p ℚ_p^×/(ℚ_p^×)²`** — a rational that is a square in every
   `ℚ_p` is a square. Equivalently: a squarefree integer `≠ 1` is a quadratic nonresidue modulo
   infinitely many primes. This is Dirichlet plus the Jacobi symbol, and Mathlib has both.
2. **surjectivity of `ℚ^× → ℚ_p^×/(ℚ_p^×)²`**, i.e. every local square class contains a rational,
   plus the simultaneous (weak approximation) version over a finite set of places.

The existence theorem is now in the tree, in `Global/Existence.lean` (disjoint supports) and
`Global/ExistenceGeneral.lean` (the general form, obtained by a twist). Downstream of it:

* the **three-square theorem**, `Global/ThreeSquares.lean`, via the local three-squares statements
  and the Davenport–Cassels descent;
* the **Hasse principle for diagonal quaternary forms**, `Global/QuaternaryForms.lean`: the two
  binary halves share a value exactly when a pair of Hilbert-symbol families is prescribable, and
  the product formula for those families is reciprocity;
* the **Hasse principle for diagonal quinary forms**, `Global/QuinaryForms.lean`: here the shared
  value cannot be prescribed by Hilbert symbols alone, and the argument instead approximates the
  local values by a single integral pair of coordinates (`Global/IntApprox.lean`), which lands the
  resulting rational value in the correct square class at every relevant place; away from those
  places the ternary half has unit coefficients and is isotropic outright
  (`Global/OddUnitIsotropy.lean`), so the quaternary principle finishes the job.

* the **Hasse–Minkowski theorem for a diagonal form in an arbitrary number of variables**,
  `Global/DiagHasse.lean`. A form is recorded by a family of coefficients `a : Fin n → ℚ`, and
  `Global/DiagForm.lean` / `Global/DiagSplit.lean` supply the two structural facts the induction
  needs: an isotropic form with invertible coefficients is universal, and a form in at least three
  variables is isotropic exactly when its binary head and its tail represent a common nonzero
  value. `Global/DiagBase.lean` reads the principles in `n ≤ 4` variables in that language, and
  the induction step for `n ≥ 5` is the five-variable argument verbatim: the tail, enlarged by the
  shared rational value, has `n - 1` variables and the induction hypothesis applies to it.
* `Global/DiagRepr.lean` drops the hypothesis that the coefficients be invertible (a form with a
  vanishing coefficient is isotropic outright) and recasts the theorem as the Hasse principle for
  the **representation of a prescribed rational number** by a diagonal form, which is the shape in
  which it is normally used.

Serre's chapter on the Hasse–Minkowski theorem is therefore complete, for diagonal forms; since
every quadratic form over a field of characteristic other than two is diagonalisable, this is the
theorem itself, modulo a diagonalisation step that is pure linear algebra. None of this touches
the two blockers of §2; it is a self-contained extension of the degree-two theory.
