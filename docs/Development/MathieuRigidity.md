# The rigidity method on the Mathieu groups

This note records a complete, machine-checked-where-possible survey of what the **rigidity
method** does and does not give for the five Mathieu groups `M₁₁, M₁₂, M₂₂, M₂₃, M₂₄`, together
with the state of the corresponding Lean formalization.

The group-theoretic side of the survey was computed twice, independently:

* **character theory** — structure constants from the GAP Character Table Library
  (`ctbllib` 1.3.11) plus a generation test against every class of maximal subgroups
  (a triple fails to generate iff it can be realised inside some maximal subgroup, and the
  number of such triples is again a sum of structure constants, read through the stored class
  fusion maps);
* **brute force** — explicit permutation computation inside `M₁₁ ≤ S₁₁` and `M₁₂ ≤ S₁₂`
  (full enumeration of the group, of its conjugacy classes, of every product-one triple, and of
  the subgroup generated by each such triple).

The two agree exactly wherever both are available. For `M₂₂, M₂₃, M₂₄` only the character-theoretic
computation is feasible; where a maximal subgroup's table is missing from the library the
contribution is forced to `0` by the stored fusion map (the missing class simply has empty
preimage), so no gap is papered over.

## The criterion used

For a triple `C = (C₁, C₂, C₃)` of conjugacy classes of a finite group `G` put

```
N(C) = #{ (x, y, z) ∈ C₁ × C₂ × C₃ : x y z = 1 }
     = (|C₁||C₂||C₃| / |G|) · Σ_χ χ(c₁) χ(c₂) χ(c₃) / χ(1).
```

`C` is **rigid** when `Z(G) = 1`, `N(C) = |G|`, and every product-one triple in `C` generates `G`.
Equivalently — this is the form the Lean development uses,
`InverseGalois/Rigidity/StructureCount.lean` — for one fixed `z ∈ C₃` the fibre

```
F(z) = { x ∈ C₁ : x⁻¹ z⁻¹ ∈ C₂ }
```

has exactly `|C_G(z)|` elements and each of them generates `G` together with `z`; then the
product-one triples form a single orbit under simultaneous conjugation, of size `|G|`.

Rigidity of `C` gives a regular Galois realization of `G` over `K(T)`, where `K` is the fixed
field of the subgroup of `(ℤ/exp G)ˣ` stabilising the multiset `C` under `Cᵢ ↦ Cᵢ^k`. `K = ℚ`
exactly when the triple is **rationally rigid**.

## Results

### `M₁₁`, order 7920, 10 classes

Fourteen triples have `N(C) = |G|`; exactly **four are rigid**:

| triple | rigid | field of definition |
|---|---|---|
| `(2A, 4A, 11A)` | ✔ | `ℚ(√−11)` |
| `(2A, 4A, 11B)` | ✔ | `ℚ(√−11)` |
| `(2A, 11A, 11A)` | ✔ | `ℚ(√−11)` |
| `(2A, 11B, 11B)` | ✔ | `ℚ(√−11)` |

The ten non-rigid candidates are `(2A,2A,5A)`, `(2A,2A,6A)`, `(2A,3A,3A)`, `(2A,3A,4A)`,
`(2A,3A,11A/B)`, `(2A,8A,8B)`, `(2A,11A,11B)`, `(3A,3A,11A/B)`. Note `(2A,8A,8B)` and
`(2A,11A,11B)` *are* stable under the cyclotomic action — they are rational as multisets — but
their product-one triples all land in a proper subgroup.

### `M₁₂`, order 95040, 15 classes

Thirty-three triples have `N(C) = |G|`; exactly **eight are rigid**:

`(2A,3A,11A)`, `(2A,3A,11B)`, `(2A,4A,11A)`, `(2A,4A,11B)`,
`(2A,4B,11A)`, `(2A,4B,11B)`, `(2B,3B,11A)`, `(2B,3B,11B)`,

all with field of definition `ℚ(√−11)`. (Class labels follow the ATLAS conventions
`|2A| = 396`, `|2B| = 495`, `|3A| = 1760`, `|3B| = 2640`, `|4A| = |4B| = 2970`, normalised by
`8A² = 4A`; in the degree-12 permutation representation the cycle types are `2A = 2⁶`,
`2B = 2⁴1⁴`, `3A = 3³1³`, `3B = 3⁴`, `4A = 4²1⁴`, `4B = 4²2²`.)

### `M₂₂`, order 443520, 12 classes

Four candidates — `(2A,2A,5A)`, `(2A,3A,11A)`, `(2A,3A,11B)`, `(2A,4A,4B)` — and **none is
rigid**: every product-one triple in each of them lies in a proper subgroup.

### `M₂₃`, order 10200960, 17 classes

Three candidates — `(2A,3A,3A)`, `(2A,3A,11A)`, `(2A,3A,11B)` — and **none is rigid**.
The rational candidate `(2A,3A,3A)` fails generation with a large margin (1 782 720 product-one
triples inside maximal subgroups).

This is the expected answer. `M₂₃` was the last of the 26 sporadic groups to be realised as a
Galois group over `ℚ`, in

> X. Huang, B. Jackson, K.-H. Lee, B. Poonen, R. Pries, S. Zhang,
> *The Mathieu group `M₂₃` is a Galois group over `ℚ`*, arXiv:2608.08538, 9 August 2026,

and precisely *not* by rigidity: the authors use a **non-rigid** triple of conjugacy classes
together with a numerical Belyi-map computation, producing an explicit degree-23 polynomial over
`ℚ`. The smallest positive Nielsen class they find, `{2A, 23A, 23B}`, has `|Ni_c| = 7`.

### `M₂₄`, order 244823040, 26 classes

Twenty-nine candidates; exactly **eight are rigid**:

| triple | field of definition |
|---|---|
| `(2A, 3B, 23A)`, `(2A, 3B, 23B)` | `ℚ(√−23)` |
| `(2B, 3A, 23A)`, `(2B, 3A, 23B)` | `ℚ(√−23)` |
| `(2A, 4A, 21A)`, `(2A, 4A, 21B)` | `ℚ(√−7)` |
| `(2B, 3A, 21A)`, `(2B, 3A, 21B)` | `ℚ(√−7)` |

Every rational candidate fails the generation test, e.g. `(2A,3B,11A)` and `(2B,3A,11A)` each
leave 190 080 product-one triples inside maximal subgroups, and `(3A,3B,4A)` leaves 138 240.

## The headline

**No Mathieu group has a rationally rigid triple.** Every rigid triple found above has
stabiliser of index exactly `2` in `(ℤ/exp G)ˣ`, so the rigidity method places these groups over
a *quadratic* rational function field and no lower:

| group | rigid triples | smallest field reached by rigidity |
|---|---|---|
| `M₁₁` | 4 | `ℚ(√−11)` |
| `M₁₂` | 8 | `ℚ(√−11)` |
| `M₂₂` | 0 | — |
| `M₂₃` | 0 | — |
| `M₂₄` | 8 | `ℚ(√−7)` and `ℚ(√−23)` |

This is consistent with the literature: the classical realizations of `M₁₁`, `M₁₂`, `M₂₂`, `M₂₄`
over `ℚ` do not come from rigidity of a single rational triple but from *weaker* rationality
criteria (rational rigidity of a Galois-stable multiset combined with a braid/Hurwitz argument,
or a rigid triple over a quadratic field followed by a descent), and `M₂₃` needed a genuinely
different method.

## What is formalized

Two of the twelve rigid triples above are certified in the Lean kernel, on top of the `Mathieu`
library in this repository: `(2A, 4A, 11B)` of `M₁₁` and `(2A, 3A, 11A)` of `M₁₂`.

### `M₁₁`

`InverseGalois/Rigidity/Examples/MathieuM11.lean`. Its capstone is

```lean
theorem rigid_triple : Nat.card ↥(rigidTuples classTriple) = Nat.card ↥M11
```

with `#print axioms` giving exactly `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`. Concretely, with
`z = m11a` the standard 11-cycle and

```
x = m11a · m11b⁻¹ · m11a⁻³ · m11b² · m11a²
```

the pair `x, z` generates `M₁₁` and the structure-constant fibre has exactly
`11 = |C_{M₁₁}(z)|` elements.

The kernel computation is organised so that nothing needs `native_decide`:

* permutations are handled as base-11 integer codes (`Mathieu.EnumM11.enc/dec/φ`), with a single
  new primitive `mulC` for the product of two codes;
* the class `2A` is a binary-search-tree literal `TX` of 165 keys, and `decide +kernel` checks
  only that `TX` is closed under conjugation by the two generators and their inverses; the
  one-sided soundness of `Btree.mem` then gives `2A ⊆ TX`, which is all an *upper* bound on the
  fibre needs;
* the class `4A` needs no tree: it is the set of elements of order 4, and the test
  `q² ≠ 1 ∧ q⁴ = 1` is three code multiplications.

### `M₁₂`

`InverseGalois/Rigidity/Examples/MathieuM12.lean` does the same for `(2A, 3A, 11A)`, with
capstone

```lean
theorem rigid_triple : Nat.card ↥(rigidTuples classTriple) = Nat.card ↥M12
```

and the same three axioms. Here `z = m12a` is the standard 11-cycle, `x` is the involution

```
x = m12b⁻¹ · m12c · m12a⁻¹ · m12b² · m12a⁻¹ · m12c · m12a⁻¹
```

of cycle type `2⁶`, and `y = x⁻¹ z⁻¹` has order 3 and cycle type `3³1³`. The pair `x, z`
generates `M₁₂` — the other two generators are exhibited as the words

```
m12b = z⁻⁵ · x · z² · x · z⁻² · x · z · x · z⁻¹ ,      m12c = x · z⁴ · x · z⁻⁴ · x
```

— and the fibre `{ w ∈ 2A : w z⁻¹ ∈ 3A }` has exactly `11 = |C_{M₁₂}(z)|` elements.

The base-11 code layer of `Mathieu.EnumM11` is mirrored at base 12 (`enc12`, `dec12`, `φ12`,
`mulC12`, `dg12`), and class `2A` becomes a 396-key search-tree literal checked closed under
conjugation by the three generators. The delicate point is the second class: `3A = 3³1³` and
`3B = 3⁴` both consist of elements of order 3, so an order test alone leaves 22 candidates
rather than 11. Rather than bridge integer codes to `Equiv.Perm.support.card` — or build a
second, 1760-key tree — the two classes are separated by **having a fixed point**, a test that
is one `List.range 12` scan on the code and is manifestly conjugation-invariant. Only the easy
direction is needed: every conjugate of the chosen `y` passes, which bounds the fibre from
above.

### Fields of definition

Because `11A`/`11B` are not rational in `M₁₁` or `M₁₂`, neither triple assembles into a
`RigidityCertificate` — that structure demands each class be fixed by the whole cyclotomic
action, which is exactly the `ℚ`-rationality the survey above rules out for every Mathieu group.
Turning either `rigid_triple` into a realization would require generalising the certificate and
the descent tower in `InverseGalois/Rigidity/RET/Descent/` from `ℚ` to a number-field base; the
result would be regular realizations of `M₁₁` and `M₁₂` over `ℚ(√−11)(T)`.

`M₂₄` is out of reach for the same style of certificate: its class `2A` has 11385 elements and
base-24 codes are integers of about 33 digits.

## Reproducing the survey

The computations are not part of the Lean build. They were run against
[`ctbllib` 1.3.11](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Thomas.Breuer/ctbllib/) with a small
standalone parser for the GAP `.tbl` format (`MOT`/`ALF`/`ARC` records, `GALOIS`/`TENSOR`
character compression, `ConstructPermuted` derived tables) plus the two brute-force scripts for
`M₁₁` and `M₁₂` described above. Any GAP session reproduces the same numbers with
`ClassMultiplicationCoefficient` and `Maxes`.
